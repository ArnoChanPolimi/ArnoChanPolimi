# ENSEA · Cergy, France

`Erasmus exchange — Networks, Telecommunications & Security · Sep 2025 – Jan 2026`

<img align="right" width="345" src="../assets/photos/ENSEA/ENSEA_snowday.jpg" alt="A snow day at ENSEA">

Even now, my exchange semester at ENSEA reads back to me like something I dreamed rather than something I lived. I can scarcely believe I was there at all — a boy from a far corner of China, set down one autumn in a small French town he had never once heard of, watching the first snow settle over a campus that was never meant to be any part of his story. Some doors you do not so much choose to walk through as fall through, gratefully, at the exact moment you happen not to be looking; and that entire season ran on the same steady current of quiet disbelief.

<br clear="all">

<img align="left" width="215" src="../assets/photos/ENSEA/LaGrange_ENSEA.jpg" alt="La Grange, home for my first week">

It began, fittingly, without a roof over my head. For my first week a classmate from the student union simply took me in — his family's old house, La Grange, with a low stone cellar beneath it where we threw parties deep into the night and danced until the very walls seemed to breathe. I have never forgotten a single hour of that week: the unfamiliar warmth of a stranger's table, the wine and the laughter in a language I was still stumbling through, the strange tenderness of being made so welcome so far from anyone I knew. It felt like a tale from another century — some village boy improbably carried across the world and taken in without a second thought. Slowly, the strangeness wore into something close to home. I made friends among the French students, patient with my faltering French and generous with their own English, and learned that warmth, when you are far from everything familiar, asks for no shared language at all. I was the foreigner in every room I entered, and yet somehow it never once felt like exile — only like being the newest thread in a cloth that kept quietly making room for me.

<br clear="all">

ENSEA taught in a way I had never met before: the grande-école model, severe and exacting, built on the plain and slightly terrifying assumption that you will rise to meet it. I loved every difficult hour of it. I spent the term deep among networks — their theory in the mornings, their laboratories in the long afternoons — wiring, breaking, and patiently mending small worlds of my own until they behaved. And I leaned, without a shred of apology, on GPT, the tireless tutor that so many of us can no longer quite do without.

But what I came to love most was the town itself, that quiet place tucked into the northwest folds of Paris. I can still see the RER sliding toward the city through the early winter dark, row upon row of little houses slipping past the window, each one holding its own small lit square of warmth against the cold. I would sit with my forehead nearly against the glass and make myself a quiet promise — that one day, somewhere, one of those lit windows would be mine. I came home from France with a heavier suitcase and a lighter, braver heart, and that promise still folded carefully inside it.
