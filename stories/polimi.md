# Politecnico di Milano

`M.Sc. Telecommunication Engineering · Milan, Italy · 2024–2026`

<img align="right" width="250" src="../assets/photos/Polimi/Polimi_1stBuilding.jpg" alt="Politecnico di Milano">

By a long chain of happy accidents I found myself at Politecnico di Milano, enrolled in Telecommunication Engineering — the first chapter of my life to be written entirely beyond the borders of China. I had braced myself for the ache of being a stranger, for the slow attrition of living in a language not my own, for months of quietly counting the days until I might go home again. None of it came to pass. Almost from the very first week, everything settled improbably into place: the easy cadence of the lectures, the shape of the streets that soon seemed to learn my footsteps by heart, the particular gold of the late-afternoon light as it slanted between the old arcades. Some cities take years to befriend, and exact from you a toll for every inch of belonging. Milan was not among them. It simply held its door open and let me walk straight in, as though it had been expecting me all along.

<br clear="all">

<img align="left" width="250" src="../assets/photos/Polimi/Duomo_Milano.jpg" alt="Duomo di Milano">

And what a place it was to be let into. On clear mornings the Alps still ambush me from the far end of some ordinary street — impossibly white, impossibly near, as though a hand had propped the whole sky upright on the horizon overnight. Lake Garda opens so wide and so deeply blue that the mind forgets it has a farther shore; stand a while at its edge and you would swear you were looking out upon the open sea. Even the Duomo, that vast cliff of carved and fretted stone, refuses to hold still, trading one shade of white for another with each passing hour of the day. There is a lightness to living in the midst of all this that I never knew I had been missing — a quiet conviction that even the heaviest of weeks can be carried lightly, so long as the world beyond the window keeps insisting on looking like a painting.

The work has lifted me quite as much as the scenery. My professors here are satisfied by no result until it has been chased all the way back to its first principle, and that stubborn patience has, over the months, quietly become my own. My English has grown surer week upon week, until one unremarkable afternoon I caught myself thinking in it, dreaming in it; the instruments of the trade now find my hand before I have thought to reach for them. Measured against the anxious boy who once left Beijing, I can at last see the distance I have travelled — not merely more capable, but somehow more nearly myself. These days I live inside my master's thesis, down in the place where the machines that train vast models must speak to one another across the gulf between data centers. The problem is simple to utter and all but endless to solve: make that conversation faster, so that a run may spend more of its short life learning and less of it idling on the network. It is one great difficulty built of a hundred smaller ones I cannot yet unravel — which is precisely why I love it. I have never once wished to arrive; arrival is only a kind of ending, and I am not finished. All I have ever wanted is to keep climbing.
