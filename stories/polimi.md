# Politecnico di Milano

`M.Sc. Telecommunication Engineering · Milan, Italy · 2024–2026`

<img align="right" width="340" src="../assets/photos/Polimi/Riva_Di_Garda.jpg" alt="Riva del Garda">

By a series of happy accidents I found myself at Politecnico di Milano, enrolled in Telecommunication Engineering — the first chapter of my life to be written outside China. I had braced myself to feel like a stranger. Instead, almost from the very first week, everything simply fit: the rhythm of the lectures, the shape of the streets, the particular gold of the light between the old arcades. Some places take years to befriend. Milan simply opened its door and let me walk straight in.

And what a place to be let into. On clear mornings the Alps still ambush me from the ends of streets, impossibly white and near. Lake Garda opens so wide and blue that you forget it has a far shore at all. Even the Duomo, that vast cliff of carved white stone, seems to shift its colour with the hour. There is a lightness to living here that I never knew I had been missing — a quiet sense that even the heaviest week can be carried easily, so long as the world outside the window keeps looking like this.

<p align="center">
  <img width="235" src="../assets/photos/Polimi/GPU_set.jpg" alt="Thesis compute setup">

</p>

The work has lifted me just as much. My professors are unhappy with any result until it has been chased all the way back to its first principle, and that patience has slowly rubbed off on me. My English grows surer by the month; the tools of the trade now find my hand before I have to reach for them. These days I am living inside a master's thesis on large language models — one large problem made of many smaller ones I cannot yet solve, which is precisely why I love it. I have never once wanted to arrive. I only ever wanted to keep climbing.
