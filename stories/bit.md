# Beijing Institute of Technology · 2019–2023

B.Sc. in Electronic Information Engineering · School of Information and Electronics

<p align="justify">I enrolled at Beijing Institute of Technology in September 2019 to study Electronic Information Engineering.</p>

<p align="justify"><a href="../assets/photos/BIT/DSC_6534.JPG"><img align="right" hspace="16" width="40%" src="../assets/photos/BIT/DSC_6534.JPG" alt="BIT campus by the water" title="BIT campus by the water"></a>For the first two years, I was unsure which direction I wanted to pursue, or even which subjects interested me most. I explored several areas within the degree, including communications, electromagnetics, and signal-processing algorithms. I had not yet settled on a particular field.</p>

<br clear="all">

## Home in Gansu

<p align="justify"><a href="../assets/photos/BIT/Yellow_River_Lanzhou.jpg"><img align="right" hspace="16" width="42%" src="../assets/photos/BIT/Yellow_River_Lanzhou.jpg" alt="The Yellow River in Lanzhou" title="The Yellow River in Lanzhou"></a>Before Beijing, home was not an abstract origin. It had a river, winter festivals, mountain light, and streets where memory felt close to the ground. I did not carry those places as decoration; I carried them as a measure of distance. They made Beijing feel enormous at first, but they also made every new step more concrete.</p>

<p align="justify">The colors of Gansu were very different from the gray speed of Beijing. Shehuo, the Yellow River, and the dry air of Lanzhou belonged to a world where tradition was not a museum word; it was sound, clothing, dust, and people standing close together in the street. I think that is why I was always sensitive to place. A city was never only a city to me. It was light, texture, weather, and the way a person learns to breathe inside it.</p>

<br clear="all">

## My first competition

<p align="justify"><a href="../assets/photos/BIT/BIT_Dragon_Warrior_Arduino_Car_Overview.jpg"><img align="right" hspace="16" width="46%" src="../assets/photos/BIT/BIT_Dragon_Warrior_Arduino_Car_Overview.jpg" alt="The Arduino car assembled with my teammates" title="The Arduino car assembled with my teammates"></a>An Arduino car competition was my first experience of this kind of project. My teammates and I assembled a remote-controlled car and added components, including infrared and ultrasonic modules. We worked on controlling its movement, following a track, and using motors to launch small projectiles.</p>

<p align="justify">Looking back, the individual modules were fairly basic. At the time, though, it was our first attempt to assemble everything ourselves, and getting the parts to work together was a substantial challenge.</p>

<p align="justify">We searched online for information, wrote code, and kept testing and debugging as a team. There was a lot to figure out, but it was also a fun beginning: we were building something together and learning how to make it work.</p>

<br clear="all">

## My undergraduate thesis

<p align="justify">Later, I completed my undergraduate thesis under the guidance of <strong>Prof. Qin Zhang</strong> and the team. The topic was <em>multi-node cooperative directional transmission in wireless ad hoc networks</em>.</p>

<p align="justify">I studied how separate wireless nodes could coordinate their transmissions so that their signals combined in a chosen direction. The work involved two connected problems: synchronizing the nodes in time and frequency, and using feedback to adjust their transmissions for distributed beamforming. I evaluated these methods through simulations.</p>

<p align="justify">I graduated in June 2023 with a B.Sc. in Electronic Information Engineering.</p>
